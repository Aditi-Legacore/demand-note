B.
GENERAL DAMAGES
In accordance with California law, Mr. Freer is entitled to general damages representing pain
and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life including missed time from leisure activities,
inconvenience, fright and fear, all proximately caused by the accident. Following the collision,
Mr. Freer experienced severe daily back pain that significantly interfered with his ability to
engage in everyday activities.  He was no longer able to ride his bicycle, work in the garden,
or participate in any tasks requiring extended periods of standing.  These physical limitations
affected not only his physical health but also his overall lifestyle and emotional well-being. As
a result of being rear-ended, Mr. Freer developed a profound fear of driving, particularly on
freeways. This fear severely restricted his mobility, preventing him from surfing or traveling
out of town—activities that had previously been central to his sense of identity and joy. The
sudden inability to participate in these leisure activities caused him to suffer from severe
depression and anxiety. Moreover, Mr. Freer was unable to return to his long-standing career
in construction, a profession he had been dedicated to for nearly a decade. The uncertainty
surrounding his ability to resume work left him with a sense of hopelessness about his future
and financial stability. The physical pain, emotional trauma, and lifestyle disruption he
endured were significant and ongoing. These injuries and their consequences deeply affected
nearly every aspect of Mr. Freer’s personal and professional life.
